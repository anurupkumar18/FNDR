# Conversations with knowledge workers

Short, structured conversations with people outside the team. Several tickets write here in the same format: PD-18 (finding things and getting back into work), PD-19 (quick actions and automations), PD-20 (refinding files and pages), PD-21 (voice and first impressions). This file has the PD-18 guide; the other tickets reuse the consent note, the rules, and the note template, and add their own focus questions.

**This repo is public.** Notes never contain a name, an employer, a school, a client, a product they work on, a URL, a file name, or anything else that could identify the person or their work. No screenshots, audio, or video.

## Rules for the interviewer

- Ask about the last time something happened, not what they would do. "Tell me about the last time" beats "Would you use".
- Do not pitch, explain, or defend FNDR. No demo until the end, and only if they want one.
- Do not lead. Not "Is it annoying when...", but "What happened next?"
- Let silence sit. Follow the most specific story.
- Write their exact words in quotes when they say something sharp. Strip anything identifying.
- 20 minutes. Stop on time.

## Consent note (read aloud before starting)

> We are students building a Mac app for a class project. For about 20 minutes we would like to hear how you find things you have seen before, how you get back into work after an interruption, and which AI tools you use. We take written notes only: no recording, and we will not look at your screen. The notes do not include your name, your employer, or anything that identifies you, and they will be stored in a public code repository. You can skip any question or stop at any time. At the end, if you want, we can show you what we are building. Is that OK?

Record "consent: yes" in the notes. If the answer is anything else, do not take notes.

## Interview guide (PD-18, 20 minutes)

| Minutes | Part | Questions | Listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 to 2 | Consent and warm-up | Read the note. "What kind of work do you do, in a sentence?" "Which apps are open on your Mac on a normal day?" | Role category only (student, office, project lead, other); the number and kinds of apps |
| 2 to 8 | Finding something you saw | "Tell me about the last time you tried to find something you had seen before: a page, a message, a number, a file." Then: "What did you remember about it?" "Where did you look first? Then?" "How long did it take?" "Did you find it? What did you do if not?" | What they remember (words, meaning, app, time, who), the tools they reach for (browser history, Spotlight, search inside an app, asking someone), time lost, giving up |
| 8 to 14 | Getting back into work | "Think of the last time you were interrupted in the middle of something. Walk me through getting back into it." Then: "How did you know where you were?" "What did you have to reopen?" "How long until you were back in flow?" "Do you leave yourself notes, tabs, or reminders?" | Their own recovery tricks, how long recovery takes, what they reopen, what they forget |
| 14 to 18 | AI tools | "Which AI tools do you use, and for what?" "How do you give it the background it needs?" "What do you find yourself explaining again and again?" "Would you let an AI tool see what is on your screen? What would it need to show you first?" | ChatGPT, Claude, Cursor, Copilot, others; copy and paste as context; repeated explanations; trust conditions and hard limits |
| 18 to 20 | Close | "If one thing about finding or getting back into your work were fixed tomorrow, what would it be?" "Anything I should have asked?" Then offer: "Want to see what we are building? It takes two minutes." | Their words for the problem; whether the demo is wanted |

If they want the demo, show it last and write their reaction in its own section of the notes, separate from what they said before.
