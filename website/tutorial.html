User tutorial

From install to finding it again.

Seven short steps. Each one is something you can do in under a minute once FNDR has been running for a while.

  1. 1

    About five minutes

    Install and set up

    1. Download the DMG from the home page, drag FNDR into Applications and open it.
    2. Onboarding asks for two macOS permissions, Screen Recording and Accessibility, and says what each one is for. FNDR reads what is on screen to build memories; without them there is nothing to search.
    3. It then downloads the on-device models that search needs. Nothing else is required: no account and no sign-in.

    FNDR now runs in the background. Keep working as usual for a while so it has something to remember.

  2. 2

    Home, or Search & Ask

    Find something you saw

    1. Type what you remember into the search box on Home. Exact words work (LL-1482, a file name), and so does the gist (that article about local embeddings).
    2. Say when or where if it helps: the contract in Slack yesterday.
    3. Each result is a memory card: what was on screen, the app, and when. If nothing fits, FNDR says there is no strong match instead of showing a guess.

    Press ⌘K anywhere in FNDR to jump between destinations.

  3. 3

    From any memory card

    Go back to the source

    1. Open a card to see the whole memory and why it matched.
    2. Choose Reopen. FNDR opens the page, the document or the app the memory came from. A PDF opens at the page it was on when FNDR recorded one.
    3. If the place no longer exists, FNDR says so.
  4. 4

    Search & Ask

    Ask a question

    1. Open Search & Ask from the sidebar and type a question: When is the dry run?
    2. FNDR answers from your memories on this Mac and lists the memories it used under Sources. Select one to open it.
    3. When your memories do not hold the answer it says “Not in your memories”.
  5. 5

    Search & Ask, from the Final release

    Investigate a piece of work

    1. Type a request about a project, a document or a ticket: catch me up on the Smart Reminders launch. Press Investigate.
    2. FNDR works through it in steps you can watch: it searches, follows the work across apps, and checks its findings.
    3. The case file lists next steps on record, steps a later memory records as done, decisions, problems, and what happened in order. Every line opens the memory it came from.
    4. Press Open places to bring back the pages and files of that work. Press Add to To-dos on a step to keep it.

    An investigation needs a topic. For a whole day or week, use FNDR Review.

  6. 6

    Home

    Pick work back up

    1. Home lists your recent work as threads: the windows and pages that belong to one piece of work, with what changed since you last looked.
    2. Choose Open all on a thread to reopen its places. More than three wait for one more tap.
    3. FNDR Review shows a day, a week or a month at a glance, and To-dos keeps the tasks you add or accept.
  7. 7

    Trust & Settings

    Stay in control

    1. Pause capture at any time from the sidebar or the menu bar.
    2. Add an app or a site to the blocklist. FNDR stops recording it and hides what it already recorded from every screen.
    3. Turn on Private Mode when you do not want FNDR looking at all.
    4. Delete memories, or set how long they are kept. Privacy Activity lists every request that left this Mac, which by default is none.

What needs more than FNDR

Everything on this page runs on your Mac with nothing else installed. A few optional features in Labs, such as operating other apps by voice, use a ChatGPT sign-in that you set up in the app. FNDR says so before anything is sent.
